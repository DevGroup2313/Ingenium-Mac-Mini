## September

The shipwrights have worked all month, and the Hester is nearly ready for launching. I give the days as they happened.

### 12 September 1786

The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 17 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Liverpool is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly.

### 27 September 1786

Launched the Hester at Hotwells this morning on the top of the tide, in the presence of Mr. Wren, Mr. Boyd and a great crowd from the quay. she measured 163 tons burthen and cost £2,140 to build, which is some £140 more than Mr. Boyd's estimate, owing to the price of oak. The christening was performed by Mrs. Wren, and the men were given a barrel of beer.

### 30 September 1786

The clerk has copied the bill of lading twice, as Mr. Withers would not accept the first for the want of a seal. The warehouse in Queen Square is full of ginger, and until the Mary Ann is cleared I cannot receive any more. Your account of the candles is noted, but the sample you sent was damp, and I would not take more than 89 barrels on those terms.

## December

A letter from Mr. Withers at Exeter says that herring will be taken only at a discount of six per cent, which I have declined. I enclose the account of sales of the mahogany, from which you will see that the net proceeds were £529 12s after all charges. The price of mahogany in the Bristol market stood at £16 4s 4d the hundredweight when the Betsey came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. The Providence lay 13 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed.
We have drawn on you at sixty days for £813 17s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Amity. A letter from Capt. Lyle at Gloucester says that Madeira wine will be taken only at a discount of three per cent, which I have declined. The price of staves in the Bristol market stood at £12 6s 8d the hundredweight when the Charming Sally came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. I cannot tell you how the pimento will sell until the autumn fleet is in, for the grocers of London are buying from hand to mouth. If the logwood is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready.
