# Journal kept at Pell's Rest, St. Elizabeth, Jamaica, 1789

Dorothea Pell, widow, kept this journal for her nephew in England. The spelling is modernised.

## The weather

Made sail at daylight with a fair wind; the Union came to anchor in Savanna-la-Mar roads in the afternoon. Her people were employed in scraping the decks, overhauling the rigging and stowing the bales, and none was sick. Served the people their allowance of beef and pork, with a pint of rum between four, as is the custom of the voyage.
Spoke a brig bound for Kingston, whose master told us that indigo was dear there and the roads full of shipping. Captain Voss thought the Mary Ann sailed best with a little weather helm and her topsails reefed. Captain Ainsley thought the Amity sailed best with a little weather helm and her topsails reefed. The pumps were worked an hour in every watch, the ship being somewhat leaky in the upper works.

### The storm of August

The great storm of 17 August 1789 came in the night with a wind from the south-east such as no one living here had known. By morning it had flattened 40 acres of cane at Pell's Rest and carried off the roof of the boiling house; the negro houses on the lower side were flooded, and the road to the bay was impassable for a week. My overseer, Mr. Marlow, thinks that we shall make scarcely half our usual crop.

## Correspondence

The wharfinger asks £69 1s 7d for the landing of the rum, which I think too much, and I have told him so. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 6 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. The warehouse in Queen Square is full of coffee, and until the Industry is cleared I cannot receive any more.
I cannot tell you how the hoops will sell until the autumn fleet is in, for the grocers of Dublin are buying from hand to mouth. Your account of the ginger is noted, but the sample you sent was very fair, and I would not take more than 26 casks on those terms. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mr. Withers, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand.
