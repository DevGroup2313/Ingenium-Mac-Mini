# Memoranda of Tobias Wren

A commonplace book of lists and reminders, 1784 to 1792.

## Things to be done before the fleet sails

- The warehouse in Queen Square is full of indigo, and until the Providence is cleared I cannot receive any more.
- We have drawn on you at sixty days for £252 14s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Industry.
- The Union lay 5 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed.
- The wharfinger asks £53 3s 1d for the landing of the pimento, which I think too much, and I have told him so.
- A letter from Mrs. Dunning at Dublin says that coffee will be taken only at a discount of three per cent, which I have declined.
- The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 16 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them.

## Order of the day at the counting house

1. The mate mustered the people at four bells and found every man present, which had not happened since leaving Waterford.
2. Captain Kemp thought the Amity sailed best with a little weather helm and her topsails reefed.
3. The pumps were worked an hour in every watch, the ship being somewhat leaky in the upper works.

Freight rates moved with the season and with the news from Europe, and a rumour of war could double the cost of a voyage within a week. The Hotwells yards, a short distance below the city, were able to build for owners from Bristol, Liverpool and London alike. Insurance was dear, and the underwriters of the Exchange coffee house were slow to pay a claim that touched upon the capture of a vessel.
