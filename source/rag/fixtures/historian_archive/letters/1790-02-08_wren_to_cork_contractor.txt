Bristol, 8th February 1790.

Mr. Henry Alleyne,
Provision merchant at Cork.

Sir,

I write to confirm the contract for the supply of salt beef and butter for the Providence and the Hester for the present year. The beef is to be of the best mess quality, packed in new barrels of the Cork pattern, and the butter in firkins of not less than sixty-four pounds, the whole to be inspected by the sworn brander before it is shipped.

The Amity lay 14 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. The Charming Sally lay 9 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. I cannot tell you how the staves will sell until the Christmas fleet is in, for the grocers of Dublin are buying from hand to mouth. I cannot tell you how the pimento will sell until the spring fleet is in, for the grocers of Bath are buying from hand to mouth.

The price we have discussed is satisfactory to me, but I must insist upon prompt delivery, for the ships will not wait. If you cannot supply the quantity in the time, I shall be obliged to apply elsewhere, and I should regret that, since we have dealt together a good many years.

The warehouse in Queen Square is full of salt beef, and until the Providence is cleared I cannot receive any more. A letter from Mr. Fuller at Cork says that salt beef will be taken only at a discount of ten per cent, which I have declined. Freight to Liverpool is now £3 10s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask four per cent for the voyage. The warehouse in Queen Square is full of cotton, and until the Providence is cleared I cannot receive any more.

We have drawn on you at sixty days for £716 8s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Industry. I cannot tell you how the candles will sell until the Christmas fleet is in, for the grocers of Cork are buying from hand to mouth. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £776 4s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Amity. The Industry lay 16 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. A letter from Mr. Fuller at Bath says that iron bars will be taken only at a discount of two per cent, which I have declined.

I am, Sir, your most obedient servant,

Tobias Wren
