Bristol, 30th December 1786.

Mr. Tobias Wren,
at Clifton.

Sir,

The Hester lies at the Hotwells quay with her masts stepped and her standing rigging set up, and Mr. Boyd's foreman tells me that she will be ready for her stores by the middle of January. I have engaged Capt. Pryce as her master on the terms we discussed, and he has already begun to look about him for a mate and a boatswain who know the Jamaica trade.

The warehouse in Queen Square is full of ginger, and until the Amity is cleared I cannot receive any more. The wharfinger asks £20 11s 8d for the landing of the Madeira wine, which I think too much, and I have told him so. The price of linen in the Bristol market stood at £44 19s 6d the hundredweight when the Providence came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. The wharfinger asks £15 9s 9d for the landing of the mahogany, which I think too much, and I have told him so. The clerk has copied the bill of lading twice, as Mrs. Dunning would not accept the first for the want of a seal.

A letter from Messrs. Lyle & Pountney at Glasgow says that salt beef will be taken only at a discount of twelve per cent, which I have declined. Your account of the indigo is noted, but the sample you sent was poor, and I would not take more than 58 bales on those terms. The wharfinger asks £31 19s 11d for the landing of the cordage, which I think too much, and I have told him so. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £265 9s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Providence. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 16 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them.

The clerk has copied the bill of lading twice, as Mr. Hale would not accept the first for the want of a seal. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £825 2s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Amity. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Messrs. Lyle & Pountney, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. I have seen Mr. Gadsden at the Exchange, and payment for the staves is promised before Christmas.

The Industry lay 12 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £866 17s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Industry. If the herring is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Waterford is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly.

I have not yet had the Kingston letters by the December packet, and I am uneasy at their delay. If you hear anything at Clifton, let me know by the next post, for I will not write to the underwriters until I am certain of the facts.

Ever yours,

J. Hollis
