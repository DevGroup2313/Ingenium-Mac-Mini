Clifton, 14th September 1795.

Mr. Josiah Hollis,
Queen Square, Bristol.

Sir,

I have gone through the half-yearly accounts with the clerk and find them correct, with one exception, which is the item for the repairs of the Providence in the spring. The shipwright's bill is for nearly twice what he told me to expect, and I wish you to see him and ask him to explain the difference before we pay it.

Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mr. Cranmer, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. Freight to Lisbon is now £4 15s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask three per cent for the voyage. I have seen Mr. Gadsden at the Exchange, and payment for the logwood is promised before Michaelmas. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mr. Withers, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. I enclose the account of sales of the cordage, from which you will see that the net proceeds were £306 1s after all charges.

I enclose the account of sales of the cotton, from which you will see that the net proceeds were £622 6s after all charges. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Liverpool is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly. A letter from Mr. Hale at Dublin says that ginger will be taken only at a discount of six per cent, which I have declined. If the coffee is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready.

Freight to Liverpool is now £3 10s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask six per cent for the voyage. The price of herring in the Bristol market stood at £67 9s 0d the hundredweight when the Providence came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. The Mary Ann lay 18 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed.

The warehouse in Queen Square is full of Madeira wine, and until the Charming Sally is cleared I cannot receive any more. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mr. Alleyne, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 18 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. A letter from Mr. Fuller at Liverpool says that ginger will be taken only at a discount of two per cent, which I have declined.

As to the future, I have been thinking a good deal of late of retiring from active business, and I would be glad to talk it over with you when you are next at Clifton. I do not wish to leave you with more than you can manage, and I think we might arrange for Mr. Fuller to come into the firm.

Believe me, dear Hollis, your affectionate friend,

Tobias Wren
