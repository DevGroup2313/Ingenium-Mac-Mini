Kingston, Jamaica, 2nd March 1784.

Mr. Tobias Wren,
Merchant in Bristol.

Sir,

I have the pleasure to inform you that the Mary Ann cleared Kingston on the 14th of February with 412 hogsheads of sugar and 38 puncheons of rum, being as full as she could swim, and that her master, Capt. Carew, expressed himself well satisfied with the stowage. The sugars are of a good grain and colour, and I have no doubt they will do you credit in the Bristol market. The rum is of the usual proof; I would not recommend you to hold it long, for the price here has already begun to fall.

I enclose the account of sales of the ginger, from which you will see that the net proceeds were £858 14s after all charges. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mrs. Dunning, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £375 10s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Providence. I have seen Mr. Fuller at the Exchange, and payment for the muscovado sugar is promised before midsummer.

You will find enclosed the bills of lading, the invoice, and my account current to the end of January, by which you will see a balance of some £212 in your favour after the charges of wharfage, lighterage and commission have been deducted. I have drawn on you for nothing beyond the ordinary.

Your account of the butter is noted, but the sample you sent was very fair, and I would not take more than 56 barrels on those terms. Freight to Waterford is now £6 10s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask twelve per cent for the voyage. The price of linen in the Bristol market stood at £44 7s 8d the hundredweight when the Union came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. The warehouse in Queen Square is full of coffee, and until the Hester is cleared I cannot receive any more.

We have drawn on you at sixty days for £131 9s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Amity. I have seen Messrs. Lyle & Pountney at the Exchange, and payment for the molasses is promised before Lady Day. Your account of the linen is noted, but the sample you sent was poor, and I would not take more than 89 chests on those terms. I cannot tell you how the cotton will sell until the Lady Day fleet is in, for the grocers of Dublin are buying from hand to mouth.

I have seen Capt. Lyle at the Exchange, and payment for the cotton is promised before Lady Day. The clerk has copied the bill of lading twice, as Mrs. Anstey would not accept the first for the want of a seal. The clerk has copied the bill of lading twice, as Mr. Hale would not accept the first for the want of a seal.

The Providence, Capt. Ainsley, is expected from Cork within the fortnight with the butter and beef; and I shall endeavour to have her sail for England before the hurricane months, though the planters are slow, as they always are, in getting their crops down to the wharves.

I am, Sir, with great regard, your obliged servant,

Alex. Grieve
