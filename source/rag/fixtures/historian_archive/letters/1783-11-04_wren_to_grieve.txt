Bristol, Queen Square, 4th November 1783.

Mr. Alexander Grieve,
Merchant at Kingston, in the Island of Jamaica.

Sir,

I have yours of the 2nd of August by the Betsey, Capt. Lyle, with the account of the crop and the particulars of the shipments you have made on our joint account. I am obliged to you for the care you have taken, and I am glad to learn that the sugars of the present year are better coloured than those of the last, for the grocers here pay a full price for a bright sugar and complain loudly of a dark one.

My partner Mr. Hollis and I have resolved to send out the Mary Ann again after Christmas, and to put on board her such goods as you advise: the iron, the coarse linens, the salt beef and butter from Cork, and as many hoops and staves as the cooper can supply. You will please to have her loaded with all despatch on her arrival, since every day she lies in your harbour costs us in wages and provisions what a hogshead of sugar would fetch at home.

The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 5 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. The price of staves in the Bristol market stood at £29 3s 4d the hundredweight when the Charming Sally came up the Avon, and I doubt it will hold. I have seen Mrs. Anstey at the Exchange, and payment for the coffee is promised before midsummer. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Bristol is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly.

The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 14 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. If the candles is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. Your account of the staves is noted, but the sample you sent was damp, and I would not take more than 78 bundles on those terms. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 13 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them.

The warehouse in Queen Square is full of iron bars, and until the Hester is cleared I cannot receive any more. I have seen Mr. Withers at the Exchange, and payment for the salt beef is promised before midsummer. Freight to Bristol is now £6 10s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask seven per cent for the voyage.

I remain, Sir, your most obedient humble servant,

Tobias Wren
