On board the ship Hester, lying at Kingston, 23rd May 1788.

To Messrs. Wren and Hollis, Merchants,
of the City of Bristol.

Sir,

We the undersigned, being the seamen of the ship Hester, beg leave humbly to represent that the allowance of provisions on the passage out was short of what was promised in the articles, the bread being full of weevils and the beef so salt that it could not be eaten without a quantity of water, which we were not always allowed.

A letter from Mrs. Dunning at Cork says that logwood will be taken only at a discount of ten per cent, which I have declined. Be so good as to send by the next conveyance a statement of what stands to our credit with Mr. Withers, for the half-yearly balance is close at hand. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Lisbon is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 19 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them.

We do not complain of the master, who has been a just man, but of the steward, who sold part of the stores at Cork, as we believe, and we pray that an inquiry may be made. We have all of us served the owners faithfully, and hope that this petition will be received in the spirit in which it is made.

If the coffee is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. If the candles is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 10 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them. Freight to Cork is now £5 0s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask six per cent for the voyage.

We are, Gentlemen, your humble servants,

(signed with their marks by fourteen of the crew)
