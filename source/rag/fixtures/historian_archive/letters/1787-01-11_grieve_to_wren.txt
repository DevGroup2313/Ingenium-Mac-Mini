Kingston, Jamaica, 11th January 1787.

Mr. Tobias Wren,
Merchant in Bristol.

Sir,

It is my painful duty to acquaint you that Captain Carew died of the yellow fever at Port Royal on the 3rd of December, after an illness of only four days. He was buried the next morning in the churchyard of St. Peter, and the officers of the Port Royal station were pleased to attend. I have taken possession of his chest and papers, which I will send by the first safe conveyance, and have paid the mate, Mr. Kemp, his wages to the day, in the presence of two witnesses.

The Providence lay 14 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. If the linen is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. Freight to Port Royal is now £4 5s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask two per cent for the voyage. If the ginger is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. I have seen Mrs. Anstey at the Exchange, and payment for the coffee is promised before Christmas.

Your account of the ginger is noted, but the sample you sent was very fair, and I would not take more than 50 bundles on those terms. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £423 19s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Charming Sally. I cannot tell you how the mahogany will sell until the spring fleet is in, for the grocers of London are buying from hand to mouth. Freight to Liverpool is now £3 10s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask six per cent for the voyage. The warehouse in Queen Square is full of cordage, and until the Union is cleared I cannot receive any more.

The ship is not damaged, and her cargo is safe in my warehouse. Mr. Kemp is a steady man, but I do not think him fit to take her home, and I should be glad of your orders as to whether you would have me engage a master here, or wait until you can send one from England. I have sounded Capt. Ainsley, who is willing to take her at the usual terms.

I cannot tell you how the logwood will sell until the winter fleet is in, for the grocers of Cork are buying from hand to mouth. Your account of the mahogany is noted, but the sample you sent was sound, and I would not take more than 28 tons on those terms. If the cordage is damp on arrival we shall claim upon the underwriters, and I beg you to have a surveyor's certificate ready. The Charming Sally lay 13 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £780 14s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Union.

I am, with sincere regret, Sir, your obedient servant,

Alex. Grieve
