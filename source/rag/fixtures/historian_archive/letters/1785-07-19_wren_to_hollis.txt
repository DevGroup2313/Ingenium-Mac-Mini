Bristol, 19th July 1785.

Mr. Josiah Hollis,
at the counting house in Queen Square.

Sir,

Since you are to be from town until Michaelmas I put down in writing what has been settled with the cooper, lest either of us forget it. I have agreed with Mr. Ambrose Tilley, the cooper of Redcliff Back, to supply six hundred staves at eleven shillings the hundred, to be delivered at the quay before Michaelmas and not a day later, the wood to be sound oak, seasoned at least a year, and free of sap. He has asked to be paid a third in advance, which I have refused, offering instead to pay on delivery in cash and not by bill.

I must beg you to look carefully at the hoops when they come. The last lot was too thin to hold, and I will not again pay for iron that gives way at the first roll. If Mr. Tilley complains, remind him that he gave his word on the matter in the presence of Capt. Carew.

We have drawn on you at sixty days for £856 8s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Charming Sally. The Charming Sally lay 10 days in the river waiting for a wind, and her master asks that his demurrage be allowed. I cannot tell you how the logwood will sell until the winter fleet is in, for the grocers of Gloucester are buying from hand to mouth.

The wharfinger asks £13 4s 3d for the landing of the pimento, which I think too much, and I have told him so. I cannot tell you how the Madeira wine will sell until the winter fleet is in, for the grocers of London are buying from hand to mouth. A letter from Mr. Gadsden at Bath says that muscovado sugar will be taken only at a discount of seven per cent, which I have declined. We have drawn on you at sixty days for £509 5s, which I beg you will honour, and charge the same to the cargo of the Hester. It is the opinion of the underwriters that the risk to Falmouth is not increased by the late news, and I have insured accordingly.

A letter from Mrs. Anstey at Liverpool says that candles will be taken only at a discount of ten per cent, which I have declined. Freight to Montego Bay is now £5 0s the ton, and the underwriters at the Exchange ask twelve per cent for the voyage. The cooper reports that the casks of the last lot were green and weeping, and that 18 of them had to be re-hooped before the lighter would take them.

Your affectionate friend and partner,

T. Wren
