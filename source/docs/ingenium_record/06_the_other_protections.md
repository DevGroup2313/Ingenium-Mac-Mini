# Ingenium record 06 · The other protections

Part of the record of Ingenium's security system, written from Ingenium 7.54's sources and brought up to 7.64 (21 November 2026). Each section names its module in `source/build/modules/` and, for the computer apps, its patch in `source/build/desktop/`.

Her security request that most of these answer (18 November 2026): "I want you to upgrade and enhance Ingenium's security system and embedded software protection. I really want everything to be encrypted and protected ... my data is protected and also my code is protected and anything that I build inside of Ingenium with my local models and all of my current apps." Her answers then: the lock asked at first start; everything on the computer's disk sealed with the same password; messages between her devices encrypted end to end; her code signed and tamper-evident, scrambled, under an owner key and sealed in Fabrica, "and any additional features that will further protect me and encrypt Ingenium and all of its content". Her word on the live preview (source/previews/security): "I approve of the preview, please commit and push it everywhere".

## 1. Settings, Security (`r318.js`, 7.36)

A room in Settings that says how many protections are on, then each, with its true state: On, Off, or Not yet (with the release it is planned for). Nothing is ever said to be on that is not.

* **Sealed on this device**: the sign-in (record 02), its ways back in read from its record, Lock now and Sign out; the sign-in's own card stays in Settings, Your data, Sign-in and reset.
* **At first start**: a copy with no sign-in asks once, after the welcome and the tour (Protect it, which opens the sign-in's own steps; Not now). Not in the demonstration edition, and not while a test drives the page (`navigator.webdriver`), unless the test asks for it.
* **More protection** (the device's settings, localStorage `ing.security.v1`, shared by every profile; defaults `{offline:false, sleep:true, slow:true, clip:true, log:true, export:false}`):
  * Offline only (off until turned on): requests from the page to anything but this device and her home network wait, with Allow for an hour and Allow once; Ingenium's own way out (`netFetch`) follows it too.
  * Lock when the computer sleeps (on): a pause in time longer than 90 seconds locks it, as the time away does.
  * Slow down wrong passwords (on): after three, each try waits longer, 2, 4, 8 seconds and so on, up to five minutes; nothing is wiped (count in `ing.security.fails.v1`).
  * Clear a copied secret (on): a password, a recovery word or a pairing code copied from Ingenium is cleared from the clipboard after a minute, where the browser lets a page do so.
  * A record of every opening (on): when and on which device Ingenium opened, and every wrong password, kept in the desk, so sealed with it.
  * Ask before each export (off until turned on; needs the sign-in; turning it off asks for the password too): section 2.
* **Not yet**, said so with the release planned: her apps' code scrambled. Ingenium itself, signed and checked, is built for the Mac and Windows apps from 7.58 (section 9); her apps signed when shipped from 7.60 and 7.62 (section 11): the check at start of an app built from its build kit comes later.
* **In the practice studio** (record 04, section 9): a line before the room says this device's protection can be read but not changed there, and that there is no sign-in; the switches' names are not written from it.
* `window.IngSecurity` gives the others `cfg()` (the switches), `set(key, value)`, `view()` (which part of the room is shown), `paint()`, `log()` (the record of every opening, kept in the desk as `state.secLog`) and the Offline only helpers (`net`, `allowed(url)`, `allow`).

## 2. Ask before each export (`r341.js`, 7.51)

* Every file Ingenium saves out of itself goes through `window.saveFileNative` (the base's own, queued one at a time by r137); this part stands in front of it.
* With the switch on and the sign-in on, a file that leaves unsealed waits for the password (`IngLock.confirm`, record 02 section 6.3): the right password lets it go; Cancel, Escape or the page locking keeps it here, and says so.
* What is sealed already leaves without the question: a backup sealed whole (it begins `INGENIUM SEALED BACKUP 1`), and the recovery key file, which the sign-in saves only after the password has just been asked.
* Files asked for while the question is open share its one answer; the next export asks again.
* `window.IngExportAsk`: `on()`, `sealed(data)`.

## 3. Sealed on the computer's disk (`r340.js` and `desktop/app_disk_749.py`, `app_disk_750.py`; 7.49 and 7.50)

* While the owner is signed in, the computer app is handed the disk key (`IngLock.diskKey()`: derived from the data key with HKDF-SHA-256, info `"ingenium-disk 1"`; never the password and never the data key itself) and keeps it in memory only (`disk_key_hold`). When Ingenium locks it is dropped (`disk_key_drop`); a page opened locked drops it too, so a refresh never leaves it behind.
* Sealed with it: the apps waiting for her devices and Fabrica's runs, their logs and files, in 1 MiB AES-256-GCM chunks (7.49); the records of waiting apps under names that say nothing, a run's record once it ends, and texts for training (7.50). A run still runs on plain files while it runs; its files are sealed when it ends.
* When the sign-in is turned off, everything sealed on the disk is opened again first (`disk_unseal_all`).
* The seal's own names in the computer app (7.57, `desktop/app_disk_fix_757.py`): `_DISK_SEAL`, `_disk_seal_status()`, `_disk_seal_write()`. 7.49 had taken three names that the drive's repositories use (`_DISK`, `_disk_status`, `_disk_write`), which stopped repositories on a drive from working until 7.57. A lesson for any app: a patch that adds a part must check that none of its names is taken already.
* Settings, Security, The computer's disk says what is sealed and what is not yet, with Seal them now; it repeats only what the computer app itself reports it seals (`disk_status`), so an older app is never said to seal more than it does.
* `window.IngDiskSeal`: `state()`, `describe(hasFabrica)`, `status()`, `sealNow()`.

## 4. Sealed between her devices (`r319.js` and `desktop/app_seal_737.py`, 7.37)

Her answer: "Yes, encrypt them". When a device pairs with a computer that seals (its hello says `seal: 1`):

* the device makes a key pair of its own (ECDH P-256; the private half never leaves the browser's store, `ingenium_seal`), sends its public half with the pairing code, and both screens show the same four words (32 bits, from both public keys); she confirms them on the computer;
* the two then hold a shared key (HKDF-SHA-256, AES-GCM 256-bit) that was never sent;
* from then on every request this device makes to that computer (models, the Cloud, runs, apps) goes sealed through `/ingenium/sealed`, without the code, and every byte of the answer comes back sealed, streams included.

## 5. Ingenium Cloud (`r255.js` and `desktop/app_cloud_657.py`, 6.57)

* A computer at home can be the hub; any Mac or Windows app can keep the Cloud for others, behind its pairing code; phones, tablets and browsers connect to a computer that keeps it. Over Wi-Fi; Bluetooth is not used, since a web page cannot exchange data over it.
* What travels: what a backup holds, except what belongs to one device (its models, the computer it borrows models from, its window, its dictation, its app library, and its keys).
* Sealed: each part is sealed on the device with AES-GCM-256 under a key drawn from her Cloud passphrase (ten characters or more; PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 rounds; the salt travels with each snapshot, so a joining device draws the same key), with additional data `"ingenium-cloud 1 " + id`, and named by a keyed fingerprint (HMAC-SHA-256). The same part always seals the same way, so only parts that changed travel, and the computer that keeps the Cloud holds only sealed parts it cannot read. The key is kept in `ingenium_cloudkey` as a key the page cannot read out, and never goes into a backup.
* Bringing work in merges as Backup's Merge does (each item by its own date; the newer wins), keeping the desk as it was on the recovery shelf first.
* Away from home: a sync file, sealed with the same passphrase.

## 9. Ingenium itself, signed and checked (`r348.js` and `desktop/app_self_758.py`, 7.58)

The Security room's promise: "each release signed with your owner key, and checked by the computer apps ... before Ingenium opens: a copy changed by anyone else refuses to open, and says which file changed." Where her owner key signs, her choice (20 November 2026): on her own device, a "Sign this release" key in her master copy, so the key never leaves it.

* **The check** (the Mac and Windows apps, at every start, before the window is made): the built page is read once, its SHA-256 and size and the version and edition it names. The build number a device build stamps into its first `<meta name="ingenium-build">` is left out, so the fingerprint is the one `BUILD_INFO.txt` lists for the release and one signature serves every device build of it. Her signatures (`<the app's folder>/signatures/*.ingsig`) are checked with the owner's public keys the app carries (`_SELF_KEYS`) for its release (`_SELF_RELEASE`), both written in by `release.py` (`self_stamp()`) at every release: ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256 over PermitCore's fixed order.
* **The three outcomes**: Signed: the page opens. Changed (a signature of hers names this release and the page is not the one signed): the window opens on the app's own page, which says which file changed, what was signed and what is there now, with Show the file and Quit; nothing of the changed page runs, and the app's own address serves none of it. Not signed: it opens, and Security says so. Her own page (6.19) is never refused, and a fault in the check never keeps Ingenium closed. A refusal is kept (the last ten) and told once Ingenium opens again.
* **Signing** (Settings, Security, Ingenium itself, master edition, with the owner's key on the device): her owner key's password is asked in a card of the room's own kind; the release's fields are signed with PermitCore's `signWith` and handed to the computer app (`self_signature_put`), which checks it with the public keys it carries and keeps it only when it is hers and names this very copy. The key and its password never leave the page; the signature is no secret. Also: Save the signature (for her other computers on the same release), Bring a signature in, Check it now.
* **Its limits, said in the room**: someone who can change the computer app itself, or take its signatures away, can stop the check; without a computer app (a browser) the room says Not yet; until 7.64 the iPhone, iPad and Fire apps did too (section 13).
* Interface: `window.IngSelfSign` (`state()`, `describe(mine)`, `check(again)`, `sign()`, `bring()`, `save()`, `put(text)`); the computer app's bridge `self_check`, `self_signature_put`, `self_signature_get`, `self_reveal`, `self_quit`. In the practice studio the calls that change something (`self_signature_put`, `self_reveal`, `self_quit`) are refused (record 04, section 9).

## 10. The folder search's index, sealed, one per profile (`rag/seal.js`, `rag/store.worker.js`, `rag/main.js`, `r197.js`, `r340.js`; 7.59)

Approved on 20 November 2026 at the cost measured on a prototype. Her choice the next day, between one index for every profile and one for each: "One index per profile".

* **Where**: the browser's private file system (OPFS), through SQLite's `opfs-sahpool` file system, in the Mac and Windows apps only (the folder search needs the computer app's folder bridge). The owner's index is `rag.sqlite3`; another person's profile has `rag-<id>.sqlite3`.
* **The move** (once): until 7.58 every profile's folders were in `rag.sqlite3`, kept apart by a reader for each. At its first open, while that file can still be read, each other profile's folders, files, passages, words (FTS5), vectors (vec0) and cached vectors are copied into that profile's own file, then taken out of the shared one; a folder the owner shares stays with her too. The word index is then merged and the file rebuilt (VACUUM), so no freed page keeps a moved passage. Nothing is read or embedded again. This happens with no key held, so no profile's rows are ever sealed under another profile's key. A sealed shared file cannot be read without the owner's key, and is left as it is.
* **How it is sealed**: `rag/seal.js` takes over the read, write, size and truncate of each file's access handle, in the store worker only. Past the pool's own 4,096-byte header, a file keeps a 64-byte head (`INGRAGS1`, the key's id, the plain size, a salt), then sectors of 4,096 plain bytes, each sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 (`@noble/ciphers` 2.4.0, carried in `rag/vendor/noble`), with a fresh 24-byte nonce, the file's salt and the sector's number bound in. Opened sectors are kept in the worker's memory (at most 192 MiB, the least recently used let go first).
* **The key**: `IngLock.ragKey()` derives it from the profile's data key (HKDF-SHA-256, info `"ingenium-rag 1"`); its id is the first 8 bytes of its SHA-256. `rag/main.js` waits for the sign-in, then opens the index with it. The key is kept in the worker's memory only.
* **When it changes**: the sign-in turned on with the index open seals it in place at once (`seal.key`); a plain index is sealed in place the first time it opens with a key. Lock now and Sign out start the page again, and the worker goes with it. Turning the sign-in off: r197 waits for `window.__ingBeforeOff` before the lock record goes, and the index is opened again in place (`seal.openAll`) while the key is still in hand; if that fails, protection stays on, so the index is never left sealed under a key nobody can derive any more.
* **Refusals**, each said as such and never taken for a damaged index (which the card would offer to start again): no key (`LOCKED`), another sign-in's key (`OTHER_KEY`), a sector changed (`SEAL_BROKEN`), the cipher missing from the app (`SEAL_UNAVAILABLE`). The pool turns an error thrown in a read into an I/O failure, so `seal.js` keeps its last error for the worker to report.
* **Removing a profile** deletes its index file (`IngRag.dropProfile`).
* **Settings, Security, The computer's disk** says the index's true state, asked of the folder search (`IngRag.sealState()`).
* **Its cost**, measured on the prototype (20,000 passages, 121 MB, headless Chromium): building about three times as long as plain; the first vector search of a session about 3.5 s, later ones 37 to 74 ms, against 0.8 to 1.4 s for the plain index.

## 11. Her apps signed when shipped (`r349.js`, 7.60; `r352.js` and the shells' check, `source/shells`, 7.62)

The Security room's promise, approved on its live preview (18 November 2026): "your owner key's signature on every package, the computer ones too, so a changed file is caught and the app refuses to open and says so."

* **Signing** (r349, the master edition, with her owner key on the device, PermitCore's own): in Fabrica's Ship, her owner key's password is asked once, in a card of the room's own kind, and the key is held two minutes after the last package, then dropped (Not now makes nothing). Every package made from an app's page carries the signature inside that page (`<meta name="ingenium-signature">`, over the page with that tag's content and the build tag `<meta name="ingenium-build">` left empty, as Ingenium's own check reads its page), so the one page, the APK, the IPA and the Mac and Windows apps all carry it. Package details has a switch for each app, on unless she turns it off. Signatures: ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256 over PermitCore's fixed order. On a device without her owner key, or with one no copy carries, what she makes is made unsigned, and Ingenium says so.
* **The web app** also carries `signature.ingsig` (every file, signed together) and a check at the head of its service worker: when the worker is installed it compares every file with the signature; a changed or missing file, and the app answers with a page that says which file, instead of opening. The first visit, before the worker is installed, opens as the browser fetched it.
* **Each device's app** (7.62): the Mac, Windows, Android (Fire) and iPhone and iPad shells check the page against the signature at every start, before anything of it runs, with the owner's public keys and the refusal page compiled into each (`shells/common/make_common.py`, from `security/owner_keys.json` and `shells/common/refused.html`). A page not as it was signed gets the shell's own page in its place, saying which file and why. The package also records that it was made signed, so a page whose signature was taken away is refused too. A release is refused by `build_page.py` if any shell was not built with the check and the keys of now.
* **As it comes in** (7.62, r352): an app brought into her library (Lector's app library; Fabrica's Bring one in) is checked first. Signed and as it was signed: kept, marked Signed by you. Not signed: kept, marked Not signed. Changed, or signed with a key that is not the owner's: a card asks, opening on Leave it out (Escape leaves it out too); Keep it anyway keeps it marked Changed. What she left out is said as such, never as a page that could not be read. Copies kept before 7.62 are checked once, quietly.
* **Check a package** (Ship card): a page or a web app's zip read here, with the owner's public keys every copy carries (`IngPermit.keys`).
* **Not yet**, said in the room: an app built from its build kit, on GitHub or in the terminal, carries the signature but does not check it at start.
* Interface: `window.IngAppSign` (`state()`, `check(u8)`, `signPage(html, ctx)`, `checkPage`, `checkZip`, `checkFile`, `packageRow`, `lineFor`, `signedNote`, `cameIn(u8, name)`, `brief(r)`, `libChip(s)`).

## 12. Opens only with your owner key (`r354.js`, 7.63)

The Security room's promise, approved on its live preview (18 November 2026): "For an app meant for you alone: it opens fully only on a device that holds your owner key, as the master edition does." Her word on its nine pictures (21 November 2026, `source/previews/owner_key`): "I approve of the previews, please commit and push it everywhere and continue working on my requests."

* **The switch** (the master edition): Package details in Fabrica's Ship has Opens only with your owner key, under Signed with your owner key, off for every app until she turns it on. Turned on, it keeps Signed with your owner key on too, since the signature carries the door. On a device without her owner key nothing is made for such an app, and Ingenium says why (it is never made unsigned).
* **The door**: every package made from the app's page carries a door, the page's first script, written in before r349 signs the page. It holds the owner's public keys and her list of withdrawn permissions as they stood when the package was made. Where the app was opened before, the app starts as it is and the opening is checked again; one that does not hold is dropped and the door comes back. Anywhere else the door stops the page before anything of the app is read, and draws itself in its place.
* **Opened with the owner key**, once per device: the key's file and its password (PBKDF2, then AES-GCM, as PermitCore keeps the key) open the key here; it signs an opening for this app and this device (`{kind:"ingenium-app-opened", v:1, app, device, made}`, ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256 over PermitCore's fixed order), which the app's own storage keeps (`ingenium-door:<app>`); the key is used once and not kept. The device is known by a random name the app keeps in its own storage (`ingenium-device`). A wrong password says so and opens nothing.
* **Opened with a permission**: PermitCore's own file, given in Settings, Licence and permissions, where the apps she keeps for herself alone are listed beside her four. It opens the app when it is signed by the owner's key, not withdrawn, within its dates, for this app, and allows its use; otherwise the door says which: Not the owner's, Withdrawn, Not yet, Ended, Another app's, Not for use. A kept permission is checked again at every start, so one that has ended closes the app. A permission names the app as its door does: its name in small letters (Tide Clock as tide-clock), or PermitCore's short name for one of her four apps.
* **Said in the room**: it keeps out a person without her key or her permission; the apps refuse a copy whose door was taken out, since the page is signed; a page opened straight from its file in a browser is not checked, and someone who takes the app apart on a device where it is open can always read it. Also: a withdrawal reaches only the copies made after it, which carry her newer list; nothing is asked of any server.
* **A page that runs only its own scripts** (a security policy without `'unsafe-inline'`) cannot carry the door: Fabrica makes nothing and says to turn the switch off for that app.
* **The shells**: what the door needs from each device's app (Web Crypto in a secure context, and a page that stops where its first script stops it) is checked on GitHub's machines (`shells/test/door_check.html`, in `.github/workflows/build-shells.yml`) on the Mac, the iPhone simulator, the Android emulators and Windows.
* Interface: `window.IngOwnerOnly` (`wanted(id)`, `door(html, ctx)`, `blocked(html)`, `packageRow(box, r, pref, signRow)`, `well(chip)`, `apps()`, `namesOf(id, slug)`); the door reads `window.__ingDoorCfg` (`{app, apps, name, keys, withdrawn}`) and sets `window.__ingDoor` while it stands.

## 13. Ingenium itself, signed and checked, part two (`r355.js`, `desktop/app_self2_764.py`, and the iPhone, iPad and Fire apps' own check; 7.64)

Shown to her as eight pictures one by one at full size (`previews/self_sign2`), and her word (21 November 2026): "I approve of the previews, please commit and push it everywhere and continue working on my requests."

* **Their copy**: the iPhone, iPad and Fire apps carry the copy `tools/app_page.py` (Fire and iPhone repository) makes of the release's page: the baked engine and the kit store left out, each block the page carries for later written beside it as `carried-<id>.txt`, and the build number stamped into its first `<meta name="ingenium-build">`. Tested against `app_page.py` itself, on a test page and on a real release page (`t_764_self2.py`).
* **The check** (at every start, before anything of the copy runs; only when a signature of hers is kept on the device, so a start with none opens at once): each file of the copy (`ingenium.html`, each `carried-<id>.txt`) read whole and hashed with SHA-256; her signatures (`signatures/*.ingsig` in the app's own files) checked with the owner's public keys the app carries (`self.json`, written in by `release.py`'s `self_stamp` at each release). * **Where it lives**: Fire and Android: `SelfCore.java` (plain Java: the reading, the signature, the states, the refusal page) and `SelfCheck.java` (the app's assets, files and refusals), `MainActivity` (the check before the page, the refusal, Check again through `IngeniumSelf.again()`), `Bridge` (`selfCheck`, `selfSignaturePut`, `selfSignatureGet`). iPhone and iPad: `SelfCore.swift` (Foundation and CryptoKit only) and `SelfStore.swift` (the bundle, Application Support, refusals), `WebViewController` (the check before the page, Check again through the `ingeniumSelf` handler), `EngineBridge` (the same three calls). * **Signing** (her master copy, on a computer): Sign this release signs the copy her computers run and the copy her phones and tablets carry with the same key while it is open (r355's `alsoSign`; the computer app's `self_mobile_fields`, `self_mobile_signature_put`), and Save the signature saves one file with both (`{"kind": "ingenium-release-signatures", "v": 1, "signatures": [...]}`) that each of her devices takes; a release signed before has Sign it for your phones and tablets. Bring a signature in on another computer takes that file and keeps both. Sign a copy you give: the full or gift edition's page chosen in the system's dialog and read by the computer app (`self_give_pick`), signed with her owner key for its computer apps and its phones and tablets, and saved as a file to give with that copy: whoever runs it brings it in once, and their apps check that copy at every start (Signed by its maker). The master edition's page is refused there.
* **Its limits, said in the room**: someone who can change the app itself, or take its signature away, can stop the check (Ingenium then says Not signed yet); an app taken away and installed again starts with no signature kept, so the signature is brought in again.
