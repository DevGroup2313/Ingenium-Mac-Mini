Ingenium 6.5 · Train a small model from zero: what was measured (24 October 2026)

llama.cpp b11321 (commit b0aca3c, the release the Mac and Windows apps carry), llama-finetune built for the processor, 3 threads
on a Linux machine; the app's own trainer code (ingenium_app.py: _train_bpe, _new_model, the trainer's arguments); a text of
the Python standard library's documentation as pydoc renders it (150,000 characters unless said). "Held back" is the loss
on the part of the text the trainer never trains on (its own split); guessing at random among 2,048 pieces gives 7.62.

1. The K and V projections (desktop/app_train_65.py)
   llama.cpp rounds a context up to 256 tokens. With the trainer's old arguments (-c 128 -b 128 -ub 128) it warned
   "n_ubatch (128) != n_ctx (256), the K and V projections will not receive gradients", for Tiny and Small alike, and an
   eight-layer model stopped on GGML_ASSERT. With -c 256 -b 256 -ub 256 every weight learns and every size runs.
     Tiny, 8 passes, learning rate 1e-4 to 1e-5:
       old (-c 128)  96 s   train 4.49  held back 6.05   (K and V untrained)
       new (-c 256) 140 s   train 4.41  held back 5.85
       new, 2e-4    155 s   train 4.69  held back 6.06
   A widened model (rig: q-F16 -> w-F16 below) trains further at -c 256 too.

2. The sizes (layers, width, heads, feed-forward width; numbers)
     tiny    4  128  4   352    1,328,256
     small   6  256  8   704    5,868,800
     medium  8  384  6  1024   15,735,168
     large  12  512  8  1408   40,645,120
   One pass over 40,000 characters, -c 256: held back 6.17 (tiny), 5.57 (small), 5.37 (medium), 5.08 (large).
   Three passes over 150,000 characters (seconds; train and held back after each pass):
     small  6e-5  132 s  train 5.87 5.19 4.86  held back 6.15 5.86 5.76
     medium 4e-5  257 s  train 5.72 5.45 5.17  held back 6.34 6.02 6.03
     medium 8e-5  368 s  train 5.80 5.89 5.67  held back 6.76 7.16 6.35
     large  3e-5  731 s  train 5.49 5.13 4.89  held back 6.25 5.89 5.83
     large  6e-5  782 s  train 6.11 6.67 5.78  held back 8.04 7.16 6.59
   So: medium 4e-5, large 3e-5 (twice as much swings and ends higher). On 150,000 characters Small does as well as the
   larger ones, which is why each size names the text it wants. Pace against Tiny (17.5 s a pass here): about 3 (small),
   6 (medium) and 16 (large); the page's estimate uses 26 s for Tiny's pass over 144,000 characters on 3 threads.

3. A model in 16 or 8 bits, widened to 32 for the trainer (the model studio's next part)
   Perplexity on 20,000 characters (llama-perplexity, -c 128): the trained Tiny 157.0152; its F16 copy 157.0157 and that
   widened back 157.0157; BF16 157.0107 and widened 157.0076; Q8_0 156.9045 and widened 156.9875.

4. A real open model trained further (.github/workflows/studio-check.yml, GitHub's Linux machine, 4 cores)
   SmolLM2 135M Instruct, 16 bits (bartowski's file, fingerprint f535f83e...), widened by the app's own code and trained by
   llama.cpp b11321 on the first 120,000 characters of Pride and Prejudice (Project Gutenberg; the illustrated edition's
   captions left out), one pass; perplexity on the novel's last 30,000 characters, which it never trains on: 23.13 before.
     1e-6   21.68   its continuation reads like the novel; in chat it still answers: "A good way to spend a rainy afternoon
                    is to take a walk along the water's edge, watching the sunset and enjoying the beauty of the landscape."
     3e-6   21.75   better read, but its chat answer wanders off into the novel's manner
     1e-5   27.80 and 28.78 (two runs): worse, and its chat was lost; 3e-5 47.0
   One pass of 120,000 characters took 996 seconds on 3 threads (the page's estimate: 0.5 x Tiny's pace per Tiny's numbers).
   llama.cpp holds a batch to its context (llama-context.cpp: n_batch = min(n_ctx, n_batch)), so gathering several batches
   before each step ("period") cannot be had with the 256-token context every weight needs: 3e-6 with eight was the same, to
   the last figure, as with one. So: 1e-6 from zero passes, one pass by default (llama.cpp reads the text in windows that
   overlap by half, so a pass reads each part twice).
