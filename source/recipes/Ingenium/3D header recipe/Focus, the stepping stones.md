# Focus, the stepping stones

Modules: modules/r291.js (Ingenium 7.00, 13 November 2026; the whole card, the night stones and the full spectrum in 7.01) and modules/r295.js (the stones' sounds, and nature sounds in the top bar).

"For the Focus page, I want the Stepping stones ... I want the roman numerals removed from the stepping stones ... I want it to look like real stones on water. I want to see the water ripple, I want the calm soothing sounds associated with it." Then: "add the same nothern lights look and effect from the Build page's diamond", "a beautiful cleary stars filled glistening sky and horizon for the night mode and a beautiful soft bright with a couple clouds landscape for day mode", "The lighter stone should be first on the Focus page on Night mode and the purple stone should be second", "I want the whole card to be filled with the purple landscape design and animation", and "I want the full colour spectrum northern lights on the landscape and horizon of the Focus page 3D header instead of the purple shade of the lights only".

## What it is

A pool that fills the whole card behind the words: a window onto still water and the sky. The stones are the steps of the task in hand: one step done stands before the step in hand, the steps ahead beyond it. By day a soft bright sky with a few clouds; by night a clear starry sky with the northern lights, reflected in the water.

## How it is made

- A WebGL shader draws the water and its ripples, the stones, the sky, the stars and the northern lights, within a fixed budget of pixels, so filling the whole card costs no more.
- By night the stones take their colours by place: the lighter stone first, the purple one second, whatever the steps.
- The northern lights run through the full spectrum (from 7.01): a rose fringe at the foot of each curtain, bright green low in the curtain, then teal and blue, violet at its top, the colour drifting slowly along the horizon. This is Focus's own: Build keeps its violet lights.
- By night the water's reflection is softened under the words, so they stay easy to read.

## How it answers

A pressed stone sounds like a stone meeting water (the sounds I approved in 7.00; two remakes were refused and the approved ones kept). Nature sounds sit in the top bar on every page.
